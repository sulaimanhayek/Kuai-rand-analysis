## How random exposure works

- **Mechanism (dataset authors):** "each recommended video in the dataset has an equal probability of being replaced by a
  random video sampled from an item pool". About 0.37% of interactions in the full logs were replaced.
- **Flag:** `is_rand = 1` on every random-log row (1,186,059 of 1,186,059). It is 0 on every standard-log row.
  [log_overview]
- **Unit of randomisation: the impression slot.** Every user gets both recommended and random items. There is no
  user-level A/B split in the data.

## Video pool

- **Freshness:** all videos were uploaded 2022-04-09 to 04-11. Video age is the same as calendar date, so a
  freshness lever cannot be tested. [video_upload_dates]
- **Creators:** 6,510 authors; 87% have one video in the pool. There is no follower count, so creator size can only be
  proxied. [videos_per_author]
- **Duration:** median 81s, IQR 32s to 148s, p95 303s. [video_duration_seconds]
- **Tags:** 46 anonymous numeric IDs. [tags]
- **Ads:** 76 videos are ads. [video_basic_overview]
- **Captions:** 7,498 of 7,583 videos (98.9%) have a caption, in Chinese. Median length is 37 characters
  (p10 16, p90 74). 912 also have cover text. [content_coverage]
- **Platform categories:** 4-level hierarchy, predicted by the platform's own model.
  - 7,571 videos have a top-level label: 38 top-level, 156 second-level and 245 third-level labels.
  - Median top-level confidence is 0.992; p10 is 0.88.
  - These are model outputs, not ground truth. [content_coverage]

## Exposure concentration and the long tail

- **Concentration:** in the pre-period, the top 1% of pool videos got 18.5% of impressions; the top 10% got 58.6%;
  the top 20% got 75.6%. 45 videos got none. [pre_period_exposure_concentration]
- **Tiers:** videos are grouped into fifths by pre-period recommended impressions.
  The tail (Q1) has 0 to 13 impressions; the head (Q5) has 179 to 9,110.

| Fifth | Random: meaningful watch | Random: early skip | Random: hates / 1K | Random: mean duration | Recommended: meaningful watch |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Q1 (tail) | 4.44s | 0.677 | 1.284 | 115.0s | 18.43s |
| Q2 | 4.81s | 0.659 | 1.321 | 112.1s | 20.27s |
| Q3 | 5.38s | 0.643 | 1.079 | 109.1s | 20.69s |
| Q4 | 6.32s | 0.614 | 1.017 | 106.0s | 21.86s |
| Q5 (head) | 8.31s | 0.568 | 0.947 | 96.1s | 23.20s |

[engagement_by_pre_exposure_quintile]

- **Under random exposure, outcomes step up with every fifth.**
  - Tail videos get 47% less meaningful watch than head videos (4.44s vs 8.31s).
  - Their early-skip rate is 11 points higher, and they get 36% more hates per 1K.
  - The recommender's early read on these videos was mostly right.
- **The gap is stable.**
  - By day, the tail-versus-head ratio stays between -0.41 and -0.51 with no trend. [tail_gap_by_date]
  - By a user's nth random impression, it moves from -0.46 (1st to 5th) to -0.49 (101st+).
    [tail_gap_by_exposure_count]
- **Tiers are confounded with content type and duration.**
  - Tail videos are longer (115.0s vs 96.1s).
  - Content mix differs by tier. Dance is 39.1% tail and 10.9% head; anime and illustration (二次元) 27.3% and 10.5%.
    Comedy is 10.9% tail and 35.1% head; short drama 8.0% and 36.8%.
  - Content types also differ in meaningful watch per random impression, from 3.05s (dance) to 8.28s (comedy) and
    8.90s (film and TV).
  - So part of the raw tail penalty is content mix. [category_l1_profile]

## Variance pilot (exploratory; the pipeline will recompute these)

- **User-level correlation, pre-period vs random period:** 0.415 for meaningful watch (0.488 impression-weighted)
  and 0.518 for early-skip rate (0.592). [pilot_user_correlation]
- **Impression-level tail vs head** (464,735 impressions; tail minus head = -3.87s):
  - SE 0.062 clustered by user, 0.143 by item, 0.146 two-way. Item variance dominates.
  - CUPED leaves the two-way SE essentially unchanged (0.1461 to 0.1458). Each user appears in both arms, so stable
    user differences already cancel. [pilot_impression_level_se]
- **Constructed user-level split** (each user coin-flipped into one arm, contributing only that arm's impressions;
  20 splits, about 26,222 users each):
  - CUPED cuts variance by 22.5% with one covariate and 28.2% with five covariates.
  - The estimate is unchanged (-3.86s in all three variants). [pilot_user_split_cuped]

## What this means for the plan

1. **Supported:** causal per-impression effects of which kind of item fills a slot. Item attributes are randomised with
   respect to users, context and time.
2. **Not supported in Pure:**
   - freshness;
   - creator size by followers;
   - session metrics (abandonment, session length), because non-pool impressions are missing;
   - a clean random-versus-recommended comparison.
   KuaiRand-1K would add full logs for 1,000 users if needed.
3. **Item text exists after all** (captions, plus platform categories to validate against). The LLM step is feasible.
4. **Content mix explains part of the tail penalty.** That makes content labels decision-relevant: the
   within-category effect depends on them.
5. **CUPED's value depends on the design.** It matters for a user-level comparison and barely at all for the within-user one.
